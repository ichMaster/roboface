## The DoD, measured

```
listen.start   +0.00 s
first audio    +0.07 s
last audio     +3.06 s
listen.stop    +3.07 s        151 frames, spread evenly over 3.00 s
```

The first frame arrived **3.01 s before `listen_stop`**, and the frames are spread rather than
bunched. The spread is the part that matters: a burst just before the stop is exactly what a device
that buffered the utterance produces, and it is what v1.3's recognition cannot work with. Device and
server frame counts agree exactly.

## Four faults, all found by measuring

None of these were visible in the code, and three had symptoms pointing somewhere other than their
cause.

| Symptom | Actual cause |
|---|---|
| A 3 s window captured 2.26 s of audio | One microphone buffer armed at a time, so the mic idled between frames. It has a two-deep queue, like the speaker; keeping both armed gives 2.86 s |
| The first hold worked, every later one was refused | `listen_stop` leaves the device *thinking* -- correct from v1.3, where recognition follows. v1.2 has neither, so it thought forever |
| Recording sounded silent; capture read 1% of full scale | M5Unified's default mic magnification of 16 is far too low for this board's ES7210. At 64, speech reads 30-70% |
| Loopback playback silent while TTS played fine | The drain was unbounded: `playRaw` returns true even when both channel slots are full, so an already-buffered recording was handed over at once and the pool buffers were recycled underneath audio the I2S task had not read |

The third is the one with the longest reach. Streaming would have "worked" all the way to v1.3 and
failed there as poor recognition -- a model problem in appearance, a one-line gain setting in fact.

## Architecture impact

**The listening window** is now a pinned contract (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §The listening window, with its
contract test): `listen_start` opens `BinaryPhase.LISTENING`, binary frames mean `audio` only inside
it, `listen_stop` closes it, and the utterance is capped in **bytes rather than seconds** -- a
property of what arrived rather than of a clock. An empty utterance is valid; v1.3 must handle zero
bytes rather than treating it as a fault.

Three device-level facts for the next phase:

1. **`endListening` ends the turn**, because v1.2 has no recognition to follow it. **v1.3 deletes
   that line** -- the comment in `main.cpp` says so at the site.
2. **`M5.Mic` and `M5.Speaker` share one I2S port and one class owns both.** Switching requires an
   explicit `end()` on the other side; `begin()` alone reports success and produces nothing.
3. **`MIC_GAIN` is a tuning constant with a misleading failure mode.** Check it before suspecting
   recognition quality.

## Not verified
