# v2.5 — GitHub Issues

Issues for phase **v2.5 — Dual microphones: direction and a cleaner signal** (version **v2 — Living
face**), derived from the per-phase Tasks in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) (§v2.5) and the contracts
in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) (§Audio, §EmotionFrame, §Gaze). This file is scoped to a
single phase; IDs continue from the previous phase (RF-072 → **RF-073…RF-077**).

The face has expressed everything except *where it is looking*, and the board has been listening with
one of its two microphones. This phase joins those: a rough left/right estimate from the inter-mic
difference drives `gaze`, and the same two channels are combined into a cleaner mono uplink. The wire
does not change — `EmotionFrame.gaze` already exists and has carried `None` since v2.2, and the uplink
stays `pcm16/16000/1`. What changes is that both finally have something real in them.

## Reconciliation notes (Step 0.5)

Read against the **real code**, not the design:

- **`Gaze` is already a protocol type** (`protocol.py`, `EmotionFrame.gaze: Gaze | None`, serialised
  only when present, `coerce_gaze` total over arbitrary input). v2.5 does **not** change that seam —
  it becomes its first real producer. `gaze=None` is written into `emotion.py::frame_for` with a
  comment naming v2.5 as the phase that gives it a voice; that comment is now due.
- **The device already carries gaze as data**: `pure/face.h` has `has_gaze/gaze_x/gaze_y`, and v2.4
  added the first behaviour — `kGazePull = 0.6f` pulls the gaze toward a hand. v2.5 adds a second
  source, so **the arbitration between them is a real decision this phase must take**, not a detail.
- **Capture is mono today**: `pure/capture.h` documents `pcm16/16000/1` with the comment *"One channel
  for now — v2.5 adds the second microphone for direction"*, and `audio_io.cpp` calls
  `M5.Mic.record(buf, samples, rate)` in its mono form.
- **v2.4's lesson governs RF-073.** Three defects that phase shipped as "working" were hardware that
  answered without functioning: a sensor on the wrong bus, then the same sensor in standby, then a
  gesture the panel cannot deliver. `/mic-info` reports `stereo=1`, which is a *configuration* flag
  and proves nothing about how many microphones are wired. So this phase's gate issue is a
  measurement, and every issue after it depends on that measurement, by construction.

## v2.5 — Dual microphones: direction and a cleaner signal

### RF-073 — Stereo capture, and proof the second microphone exists

**Description:**
Capture both ES7210 channels instead of one, deinterleave them in pure logic, and **measure that they
are actually different**. Touches `firmware/src/pure/capture.h`, a new `firmware/src/pure/stereo.h`,
and `firmware/src/app/audio_io.{h,cpp}`.

**What needs to be done:**
- `M5.Mic.record(buffer, samples, rate, stereo=true)`; the DMA buffer becomes interleaved L/R and
  therefore twice the samples for the same duration. `pure/capture.h` gains the channel count as a
  named constant rather than a `2` appearing in three files.
- `pure/stereo.h`: `deinterleave()`, `channelRms()`, and a `StereoLevels{left, right, difference_db}`
  — header-only, Arduino-free, host-tested.
- A `/mic-levels` serial command reporting both channels' RMS live, plus the **peak difference since
  boot** (the `/touch` and `/sensors` pattern: the person making a noise on one side and the person
  reading serial are not the same person).
- **The acceptance gate is a number, not a compile.** Speak close to one side of the board: the two
  channels must differ measurably. If they do not, this issue reports that and v2.5 stops here rather
  than building four issues on a microphone that is not there.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
The device captures stereo, `/mic-levels` prints two distinguishable levels, and the difference
between them is recorded as the measured basis for RF-074's thresholds.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** deinterleaving is exact for odd and even lengths, and total over an empty buffer.
- [ ] **Unit test:** `channelRms` on a synthetic signal panned hard left reports the documented
      asymmetry; on a centred signal, near-zero difference.
- [ ] `/mic-levels` reports both channels and the peak difference since boot.
- [ ] **Measured on hardware:** speech from the left and from the right produce a difference of
      opposite signs. The number goes in the issue's closing comment — it is RF-074's input.
- [ ] Capture duration per frame is unchanged (a stereo frame holds the same 20 ms, not 10).

### RF-074 — The direction estimate, in pure logic

**Description:**
Turn two channels into one number in [-1, +1] — left to right — with smoothing and a confidence
threshold, so the face turns toward a speaker and *does not* turn toward a fan. New
`firmware/src/pure/direction.h`; no glue, no clock of its own.

**What needs to be done:**
- Two independent cues, because each fails differently: the **level difference** (robust, coarse, and
  useless when the source is far) and the **inter-channel lag** by cross-correlation over the
  microphone spacing's plausible delay range (sharper, and fooled by reflections). Combine them, and
  say in the header which one dominates when they disagree.
- **Confidence, as a first-class output.** A direction with no confidence is worse than no direction:
  it makes the face twitch at noise, which is exactly what an idle desk device does all day. Below
  the threshold the estimate is `absent`, not `centre` — the same distinction `coerce_gaze` already
  makes on the server.
- Smoothing with an asymmetric time constant: follow a new speaker quickly, return to centre slowly.
  A face that snapped back on every pause would look nervous.
- Every threshold carries the physical number it came from, as `motion.h` does — the microphone
  spacing in millimetres, the resulting maximum lag in samples at 16 kHz, and why.

**Dependencies:** RF-073

**Expected result:**
`DirectionEstimator::feed(left, right, count, now_ms)` returns `{direction, confidence, present}`,
stable on speech and silent on noise.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** synthetic fixtures panned left, right and centre produce the expected sign and
      approximate magnitude.
- [ ] **Unit test:** diffuse noise (uncorrelated channels) never exceeds the confidence threshold —
      the false-positive case, which fires when nobody is watching.
- [ ] **Unit test:** a source that jumps left→right converges within the documented time; a source
      that stops decays toward centre more slowly than it arrived.
- [ ] **Unit test:** identical channels (a mono board, or a broken second microphone) report
      `present = false` rather than a confident centre.
- [ ] Compiles under `pio test -e native` — no `M5` include reachable from this header.

### RF-075 — Direction becomes gaze — locally and on the wire

**Description:**
Wire the estimate into what the face does and what the server is told. Touches
`firmware/src/main.cpp`, the renderer's gaze input, `server/roboface_server/emotion.py`, and
`specification/ARCHITECTURE.md`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **Arbitrate the two gaze sources.** v2.4 already pulls the gaze toward a hand (`kGazePull`); v2.5
  adds a voice. Decide and document the precedence — a hand at 10 cm is a stronger claim on attention
  than a voice across the room, and a rule that emerges from whichever ran last is not a rule.
- Report the direction to the server as part of the existing `event{}`/state reporting, so
  `EmotionFrame.gaze` can carry it back. The device's local reflex does not wait for the round trip;
  the server's frame confirms it, exactly as the touch reflexes do.
- `emotion.py::frame_for` stops writing `gaze=None` unconditionally. Its current comment names this
  phase; the comment gets replaced by the behaviour it predicted.
- **Seam change ⇒ ARCHITECTURE.md + contract test in the same commit.**

**Dependencies:** RF-074

**Expected result:**
Speaking from the left visibly turns the face's gaze left within a moment, both from the local
estimate and after the server's frame arrives.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Contract test:** `EmotionFrame.gaze` round-trips a real direction through encode/decode, and
      an out-of-range value is clamped rather than rejected.
- [ ] **Unit test:** the arbitration — a hand present and a voice left produces the documented winner.
- [ ] **Unit test:** an absent direction leaves the gaze to the idle drift, and does not centre it.
- [ ] `specification/ARCHITECTURE.md` §Gaze records who may set gaze and how conflicts resolve.
- [ ] Ties to the DoD: gaze turns toward a speaker within a moment.

### RF-076 — Two channels down to one, before the uplink

**Description:**
The stereo work is the device's; the server still receives mono 16 kHz at an unchanged byte rate.
Touches `firmware/src/pure/stereo.h` and `firmware/src/app/audio_io.cpp`.

**What needs to be done:**
- Downmix in pure logic, with the summing rule stated and tested: summing two correlated channels
  gains 6 dB and clips; averaging loses nothing and cannot. Say which is used and why, in the header.
- **Channel selection when one channel is bad.** A microphone that is obstructed — a hand, a case, a
  desk — makes averaging worse than picking. The rule for preferring one channel over the sum belongs
  here, with its threshold.
- The uplink frame stays exactly `kCaptureFrameBytes`: the byte rate is part of the DoD and a contract
  test already pins the frame size.

**Dependencies:** RF-073

**Expected result:**
The server sees the same mono stream it always did, made from two microphones instead of one.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** downmix of two identical channels equals the input (no gain change, no clipping).
- [ ] **Unit test:** downmix of a channel at full scale and a silent channel does not clip.
- [ ] **Unit test:** an obstructed channel (near-silent, uncorrelated) causes selection rather than
      averaging, and the chosen channel is the live one.
- [ ] The uplink byte rate is unchanged — asserted, not assumed.
- [ ] `tools/chat.py` and the fake-device integration test are unaffected: the wire did not change.

### RF-077 — Directed speech beats diffuse noise

**Description:**
Start of dual-channel VAD robustness: a voice arriving from a direction is speech; energy arriving
from everywhere is a room. Touches `firmware/src/pure/vad.h` and its endpointer.

**What needs to be done:**
- Feed the direction estimate's **confidence** — not its direction — into the VAD as a second opinion.
  Coherent energy is likelier to be a person; diffuse energy of the same loudness is likelier to be a
  fan, a street, or a laptop.
- Keep it a **bias, not a gate.** A person speaking from dead centre has low direction confidence by
  construction, and a VAD that required directionality would make the device deaf to whoever is
  sitting in front of it — which is the common case.
- The v1.4 lesson applies unchanged: the false-positive tests matter more than the true-positive ones,
  because they fire in an empty room.

**Dependencies:** RF-074, RF-076

**Expected result:**
In a noisy room the endpointer opens on speech and not on the noise floor, and a centred speaker is
unaffected.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** diffuse noise at the same RMS as speech does not open a window that speech would.
- [ ] **Unit test:** a centred speaker (confidence near zero by construction) still opens a window —
      the regression that would make the device deaf to its owner.
- [ ] **Unit test:** the existing single-channel VAD fixtures still pass unchanged, proving the bias
      did not become a gate.
- [ ] Ties to the DoD: ASR in a noisy room is no worse than the single-channel baseline.

## v2.5 scope notes

**Total effort:** roughly 17 points across 5 issues (M, L, M, M, M).
**Critical path:** RF-073 → RF-074 → RF-075.
**Phase DoD (roadmap §v2.5):** speaking from the left or right visibly turns the face's gaze that way
within a moment; ASR accuracy in a noisy room is no worse than the single-channel baseline; the uplink
byte rate is unchanged.
**Contracts pinned this phase:** `EmotionFrame.gaze` (already defined — this phase is its first real
producer, and RF-075 carries the ARCHITECTURE update and contract test), and the uplink audio frame
format, which must be proven *unchanged* rather than merely left alone.
**Model note:** chat runs on **Gemini 2.5 Flash only** (`thinkingBudget: 0`) behind the `LLMProvider`
seam; **no paid APIs** — every provider seam is mocked and a fake device drives the protocol for unit +
integration tests.

**The gate is a measurement, and that is the point.** RF-073 exists in this shape because of v2.4:
three of that phase's four post-release defects were hardware reporting health it did not have. A
`stereo=1` flag is a configuration, not a microphone. Nothing downstream of RF-073 should be written
until the two channels have been seen to differ.
