# v0.4 — GitHub Issues

Issues for phase **v0.4 — Face stub and the renderer interface** (version **v0 — Skeleton**),
derived from the per-phase Tasks in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) (§v0.4), the contracts in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) (§The face, §Device states, §Firmware architecture,
§Contracts) and the geometry in [features/DEVICE_UI.md](../features/DEVICE_UI.md) (§Layout,
§Indicators, §Screens, §Motion and timing).
This file is scoped to a single phase; IDs continue from the previous phase
(RF-017 → **RF-018…RF-022**).

v0.4 makes the screen say something. Until now it has shown a boot banner; after this a **device
state is a face**, never a word — which is the product's oldest rule and the one the whole of v2
builds on. The version also fixes `IFaceRenderer` (`begin`/`show`/`setAudioLevel`/`tick`) **before**
the real animated renderer exists, so v2 implements an interface rather than inventing one, and adds
the first **chrome**: what surrounds the face and what fades.

It closes phase v0. After this the skeleton is complete: a server, a wire, a board, and a face.

## v0.4 — Face stub and the renderer interface

### RF-018 — `pure/face.h` — the state → face recipe table

**Description:**
The mapping every later renderer obeys. A `FaceRecipe` is the small set of numbers a face is drawn
from — eye openness, mouth curve, brow angle, tilt, dimming — and `recipeFor(DeviceState)` is a
**total** function from the device state to one. Pure, header-only, host-tested; no M5GFX.

This is the v0 ancestor of v2's emotion recipe table (ARCHITECTURE §The face → Layer model and
recipes). Getting the *shape* right now is the point: v2 replaces the source of the recipe
(`EmotionFrame` instead of `DeviceState`) without replacing the idea of one.

**What needs to be done:**
- `FaceRecipe`: `eye_openness` (0..1), `mouth_curve` (−1..1, negative is a frown), `brow_angle`
  (−1..1), `tilt` (−1..1), `dim` (0..1, how much the whole face is darkened). Plain numbers, no
  drawing, no colours — a renderer decides how to express them, which is what lets v2's five skins
  share one grammar.
- `recipeFor(DeviceState)` — **total over all eight states** (reconciliation R1), including
  `kListening`, which v1 will drive, and `kBoot`.
- Recipes that match [DEVICE_UI §Screens](../features/DEVICE_UI.md): `boot` eyes closed;
  `wifi_connecting` calm; `offline` sad and **dimmed to ~60 %**; `error` the error face. `idle`,
  `listening`, `thinking` and `replying` distinct enough to be told apart across a room, which is
  the DoD's actual bar.
- **Distinctness is a property, not a hope:** a helper that says whether two recipes differ enough
  to be distinguishable, so the test can assert it over every pair rather than trusting the numbers
  were chosen well.

**Dependencies:** None — pure, and independent of RF-019.

**Expected result:**
A header-only table from device state to face parameters, proven total and proven distinct without a
board.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Host test:** `recipeFor` is **total over the eight-state enum** — every state yields a recipe,
      asserted exhaustively rather than sampled.
- [ ] **Host test:** every pair of the six DoD states (`idle`, `listening`, `thinking`, `replying`,
      `offline`, `error`) is **mutually distinguishable** by the distinctness helper. "Visibly
      distinct" is the DoD's wording; this is the part of it a laptop can check.
- [ ] **Host test:** every field of every recipe is inside its documented range — an
      `eye_openness` of 1.4 would be a renderer bug that looks like a drawing bug.
- [ ] **Host test:** `offline` is dimmed and `error` is not, per DEVICE_UI §Screens.
- [ ] No `M5`/`WiFi` include; enforced by the `native` build filter.

### RF-019 — `pure/chrome.h` — what is visible, and when it fades

**Description:**
Chrome is everything on the screen that is not the face, and DEVICE_UI's rules for it are mostly
about **time**: indicators appear when they matter and fade ~3 s after they settle, except the ones
that must not. Those rules are pure logic and belong where they can be tested with a fake clock
rather than by staring at a board for four seconds.

**What needs to be done:**
- A `Chrome` model holding the device-local facts (reconciliation R5): link state, battery percent
  and charging flag, and the current fault (an `ErrorCode` plus whether one is active). **Not**
  `EmotionFrame` — DEVICE_UI is explicit that chrome is not driven by it.
- `update(now_ms, facts)` and a `visibility()` answering, per indicator, whether it is shown —
  driven by an injected clock so tests are deterministic and instant.
- **The rules, from DEVICE_UI §Rules and §Indicators:**
  - Link shows while connecting, degraded or offline; hides ~3 s after it settles *connected*.
  - Battery shows below 20 % or while charging; hides above 20 % on battery.
  - **A fault never auto-dismisses.** It is the one thing with no timer, and the rule that makes
    the fade safe for everything else.
- **The bottom band is single-tenant**, priority `carousel > toast > level`. v0.4 has only the fault
  line, but the arbitration belongs here now — v1 adds the level meter and v2.6 the carousel, and a
  band that grew two tenants without a rule would show both.
- Fade *timings* as named constants (120 ms in, 400 ms out, ~3 s settle) so RF-021 draws to the
  documented numbers rather than to invented ones.

**Dependencies:** None — pure, and independent of RF-018.

**Expected result:**
A header-only model that answers "what is on screen right now" from facts and a clock, provable in
milliseconds on a host.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Host test:** the link indicator appears on a change and is **gone ~3 s after it settles
      connected** — with an injected clock, so the test takes microseconds rather than seconds.
- [ ] **Host test:** battery appears below 20 % and while charging, and hides above 20 % on battery.
- [ ] **Host test:** **a fault never auto-dismisses** — advanced by an hour, it is still shown. This
      is the rule the whole fade behaviour is safe because of.
- [ ] **Host test:** the bottom band yields exactly one tenant, in the documented priority order,
      when two want it at once.
- [ ] **Host test:** a fact changing *while* an indicator is fading restarts it visible rather than
      completing the fade — otherwise a flapping link would blink itself invisible.

### RF-020 — `IFaceRenderer` and `StubRenderer` — the face on the screen

**Description:**
The seam ARCHITECTURE §The face → Renderer ladder specifies, fixed now so v2 implements it rather
than inventing it: `begin` / `show` / `setAudioLevel` / `tick`. Plus `StubRenderer`, which draws
RF-018's recipe with M5GFX primitives into a full-screen sprite in PSRAM and pushes it whole.

**What needs to be done:**
- `app/face_renderer.h` — `IFaceRenderer` with exactly the four methods the roadmap names.
  `setAudioLevel(0..1)` is part of the interface **now** and does nothing until v1's lip-sync; the
  point of fixing an interface early is that later versions do not widen it.
- `app/stub_renderer.{h,cpp}` — a `LGFX_Sprite` sized to the full 320×240, allocated in **PSRAM**,
  drawn into and pushed in one operation. Drawing directly to the panel is what produces tearing;
  the sprite is why the DoD can say "no tearing" and mean it.
- Draw the recipe with primitives: eyes as ellipses scaled by `eye_openness`, mouth as an arc from
  `mouth_curve`, brows from `brow_angle`, the whole thing rotated by `tilt` and darkened by `dim`.
  No bitmaps, no fonts, no words — **a state is never a word on the screen** (MISSION, and the DoD).
- **The face occupies the central 264×184 only** (DEVICE_UI §Layout). The outer band is chrome's,
  and RF-021 relies on the face never entering it.
- Report whether the PSRAM allocation succeeded, so RF-022 can say so on serial rather than
  presenting a blank screen as a working one.

**Dependencies:** RF-018

**Expected result:**
A renderer that turns a device state into a face on the panel, in one tear-free push, behind an
interface v2 will implement rather than replace.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles clean; `pio test -e native` still passes (this issue adds no pure
      code, and must not break what exists).
- [ ] The sprite is allocated in **PSRAM** and pushed whole — no primitive is drawn to the panel
      directly.
- [ ] Every face stays inside the central **264×184**; the outer 28 px bands are untouched by the
      renderer.
- [ ] `IFaceRenderer` has exactly `begin`/`show`/`setAudioLevel`/`tick` — the interface the roadmap
      names, no wider.
- [ ] **Hardware check (DoD):** the six DoD states are visibly distinct, and the face does not tear
      while switching.

### RF-021 — Chrome on the screen — status cluster and fault line

**Description:**
Draws what RF-019 decides: the top-right status cluster (link arcs, battery pill) and the bottom-band
fault line carrying the enumerated `error.code`. Lives entirely in the outer 28 px bands.

**What needs to be done:**
- The **top-right cluster**: link as three arcs (cycling while connecting, crossed when offline) and
  battery as a pill with a fill. 12 px glyphs, per DEVICE_UI §Layout.
- The **bottom band**: the fault line — the enumerated code **verbatim**. DEVICE_UI §Screens is
  explicit that the code is shown as itself, because it is the one string worth typing into a search;
  and it is the exception to "no words on screen" precisely because it is not a state, it is an
  identifier.
- Fade in/out at the documented 120 ms / 400 ms, driven by RF-019's visibility rather than by
  timings invented here.
- **Never overlap the face**: chrome draws only outside the central 264×184. Asserted by geometry in
  the code, not by looking.
- Drawn into the same sprite as the face and pushed with it, so chrome cannot tear independently of
  what it annotates.

**Dependencies:** RF-019, RF-020

**Expected result:**
The screen shows what is happening around the face, and stops showing it when it stops mattering.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles clean.
- [ ] Chrome is drawn only in the outer bands — the central 264×184 is provably untouched by this
      module (a geometry assertion, not a visual judgement).
- [ ] Visibility comes from RF-019 — no timing literals in the drawing code, so the tested rules are
      the rules that run.
- [ ] The fault line renders the enumerated code verbatim, including a code this build does not
      recognise (`kUnknown` still prints something a person can search for).
- [ ] **Hardware check (DoD):** link and battery appear when they matter and fade when they settle;
      a fault shows the error face plus its code, and does not fade.

### RF-022 — Wiring: state drives the face, brightness, and the self-test

**Description:**
Makes the renderer the *only* way the app draws, adds screen brightness from configuration, and adds
a serial-triggered self-test that cycles every face — which is what turns "each state visibly
distinct" from an assertion into something a person can actually check.

**What needs to be done:**
- **Remove the direct `M5.Display` drawing from `main.cpp`** (reconciliation R2). Every pixel of the
  face goes through `IFaceRenderer`; the DoD requires it and today it is not true.
- Drive `show()` from the state machine's transitions, and call `tick()` from the loop.
- Feed RF-019's chrome model from device-local facts: link state from `net`, battery from
  `M5.Power`, faults from the `error{code}` frames `ws` already delivers.
- **`SCREEN_BRIGHTNESS` in `config.h`** and `config.example.h` (reconciliation R6), applied at
  startup.
- **A `/faces` self-test**: typing it over serial cycles the six DoD states with a pause on each,
  announcing the state name on serial while the screen shows only the face. Without a reachable
  server, `idle`/`thinking`/`replying` cannot otherwise be entered — and DEVICE_UI's whole point is
  that the screen never says which state it is in, so the serial announcement is how a person
  checks the screen is right.
- **The tiny corner debug line**, toggleable and off by default, per the roadmap's task.
- **Fix v0.3 code-review finding 5** (reconciliation R4): a `reply` arriving outside a turn currently
  prints while the state machine rejects the transition. Text and state must agree — and from this
  version, state is on a screen a person is looking at.

**Dependencies:** RF-020, RF-021

**Expected result:**
The board shows a face for whatever it is doing, dims to the configured brightness, and can be asked
to prove all six faces on demand.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles clean and `pio test -e native` still passes.
- [ ] **`main.cpp` contains no `M5.Display` drawing call** — the renderer interface is the only way
      the app draws a face, which is the DoD's own wording and is checkable by grep.
- [ ] `SCREEN_BRIGHTNESS` is read from `config.h`, and `config.example.h` documents it.
- [ ] A `reply` outside a turn no longer prints as though a turn were running (v0.3 finding 5).
- [ ] **Hardware check (DoD):** `/faces` over serial cycles six visibly distinct faces; a fault shows
      the error face plus its code; **v0 is a complete, self-contained skeleton**.

## v0.4 scope notes

**Total effort:** ~17 points (S=1, M=3, L=5) — the renderer is the only `L`, and the two pure modules
carry every test that does not need a board.
**Critical path:** RF-018 → RF-020 → RF-021 → RF-022. RF-019 branches off the start alongside RF-018.
**Phase DoD (roadmap §v0.4):** the board shows a distinct face for `idle`, `listening`, `thinking`,
`replying`, `offline` and `error`; link and battery appear when they matter and fade when they
settle; a fault shows the error face plus its code; the renderer interface is the only way the app
draws a face; v0 is a complete, self-contained skeleton.
**Contracts pinned this phase:** **`IFaceRenderer`** — `begin`/`show`/`setAudioLevel`/`tick`, fixed
now so v2's animated renderer implements it rather than inventing one. The `EmotionFrame` seam is
**not** touched: v0.4's face is driven by `DeviceState`, and v2.2 changes the source without changing
the idea of a recipe.
**Explicitly out of scope, by design:** no `EmotionFrame` (v2.2), no skins (v2.6), no animation or
crossfade (v2.1), no touch (v2.4), no audio level (v1 — `setAudioLevel` exists and does nothing), no
status sheet (v2). The face is a stub; the *interface* and the *mapping* are the deliverables.
**Hardware note:** three acceptance criteria can only be judged by looking at the screen. The
`/faces` self-test in RF-022 exists so that judgement is one command rather than an afternoon of
contriving states — and so it can be made without a reachable server.
**Model note:** no model call anywhere in this version; the firmware's host tests touch no network.
