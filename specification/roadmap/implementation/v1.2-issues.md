# v1.2 — GitHub Issues

Issues for phase **v1.2 — Microphone capture and push-to-talk** (version **v1 — Voice**),
derived from the per-phase Tasks in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) (§v1.2) and the
contracts in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) (§Contracts, §Audio pipeline, §Interaction),
with the bottom-band rules from [features/DEVICE_UI.md](../features/DEVICE_UI.md).
This file is scoped to a single phase; IDs continue from the previous phase
(RF-034 → **RF-035…RF-040**).

v1.1 made the device speak. This makes it hear: ES7210 capture at 16 kHz PCM16, streamed as binary
`audio` frames **while the person is still talking**, bounded by `listen_start` / `listen_stop` and
triggered by press-and-hold on the face.

**Reconciled against the real code.** As in v1.1, the vocabulary is already there — `LISTEN_START`,
`AUDIO` and `LISTEN_STOP` are declared in `DeviceMessage`, and
`device_binary_meaning(BinaryPhase.LISTENING) → AUDIO` already gives an inbound binary frame its
meaning. What is missing is the typed frames, the router state that opens the window, and every line
of the device side: **the firmware has no microphone or touch code at all** — `M5.Mic` appears
exactly once, in `audio_io.cpp`, being turned *off* so the speaker can have the bus.

**Two facts carried from v1.1's review, which change this phase's design:**

1. **PSRAM reports zero free on this board** (v1.1 finding 3, held). The roadmap text for this phase
   says "a small PSRAM chunk buffer"; that is not available. Capture uses internal RAM, which is
   affordable *because* the chunks are small and streamed — it is buffering a whole utterance that
   would not be.
2. **A new `ParseResult` must be added to `ws.cpp`'s dispatch as well as to the parser**, or it is
   discarded silently one layer below `main.cpp`. That cost half a debugging session in v1.1. This
   phase adds device→server frames rather than server→device ones, so the mirror of that trap is
   the **server's** decoder table and the router's state machine.

## v1.2 — Microphone capture and push-to-talk

### RF-035 — The listening window — `listen_start`, `audio`, `listen_stop`

**Description:**
The server side: typed frames for the window, the router state that makes an inbound binary frame
mean `audio`, and a bound on how long a person may talk. Touches
`server/roboface_server/{protocol,router}.py`, `tests/fake_device.py`, `specification/ARCHITECTURE.md`
and `tests/contract/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- `ListenStart` and `ListenStop` typed frames, their codec entries and their decoder-table rows.
  The message *names* already exist in `DeviceMessage`; what is missing is the same thing `TtsEnd`
  was missing in v1.1 — a frame type and a decoder.
- The router opens `BinaryPhase.LISTENING` on `listen_start` and closes it on `listen_stop`, and
  consumes `device_binary_meaning` rather than restating the rule.
- **A binary frame outside the window is a protocol violation**, answered with `bad_frame`. That is
  what the unlabelled framing buys and what makes it safe.
- **A maximum utterance length**, enforced in bytes rather than seconds so it is a property of what
  arrived rather than of a clock. Exceeding it ends the window with an enumerated error rather than
  a silent truncation — a person who talked too long should be told, not half-heard.
- `listen_start` while already listening, and `listen_stop` while not, are both protocol errors
  rather than no-ops: they mean the device and the server disagree about state, and that is worth
  surfacing while there is one device and one developer.
- The fake device gains an utterance helper so a test can stream PCM the way a board does.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
The server accepts a streamed utterance, bounded and framed, and refuses audio that arrives outside
a window.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `listen_start` / `listen_stop` decode to typed frames; the round trip is pinned
- [ ] Binary frames are accepted **only** between them; outside, the connection gets `bad_frame`
- [ ] An utterance over the cap ends with an enumerated error, not a truncation
- [ ] Duplicate `listen_start` and orphan `listen_stop` are refused
- [ ] **Contract test:** the window's framing and the binary-phase rule, driven by the fake device
- [ ] `specification/ARCHITECTURE.md` records the window and the cap, in this commit

### RF-036 — Firmware capture — I2S in, streamed in chunks

**Description:**
The device half of the microphone: ES7210 at 16 kHz mono PCM16, chunked and sent as it is captured.
Touches `firmware/src/pure/` (the chunk math), `firmware/src/app/audio_io.*`,
`firmware/src/main.cpp` and `firmware/test/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **Pure:** the capture chunking — how many samples make a frame at the target 20–40 ms, and the
  accounting that proves nothing is lost or double-sent across chunk boundaries. Host-tested,
  Arduino-free.
- **Glue:** `M5.Mic` capture into a chunk buffer, sent the moment it fills. **Never buffer the whole
  utterance**: v1.3's streaming recognition depends on audio arriving *during* speech, and a design
  that uploaded at the end would have to be rewritten there rather than extended.
- **The buffer is internal RAM, not PSRAM.** v1.1's review found PSRAM reports zero free on this
  board (finding 3, held); the roadmap's "small PSRAM chunk buffer" is not available. Small chunks
  make that affordable — it is buffering a whole utterance that would not be.
- **Mic/speaker switching**, the mirror of what `audio_io` already does for playback: the Core S3
  shares one I2S bus, so capture ends the speaker and playback ends the mic. v1.1 left the speaker
  releasing the bus on `tts_end`; this is the other half, and the two must not both hold it.
- Capture stops cleanly on `listen_stop`, on an error, and on a dropped socket.

**Dependencies:** RF-035

**Expected result:**
Holding the trigger streams PCM to the server in ~20–40 ms frames while the person is still talking.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] Frames leave during capture, not after it — asserted by the device's own counters
- [ ] The bus is switched to the mic on capture and released after, and never held by both
- [ ] Capture ends cleanly on `listen_stop`, on an error and on a disconnect
- [ ] **Unit test:** the chunk math and the no-loss/no-duplication accounting, under `pio test -e native`
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles; no Arduino include reaches `src/pure/`

### RF-037 — Press-and-hold — the PTT state machine and the touch panel

**Description:**
The trigger. Touches `firmware/src/pure/` (the state machine), `firmware/src/main.cpp` (the FT6336U
glue) and `firmware/test/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **Pure:** a press-and-hold state machine — idle → pressed → holding → released — with the
  **~120 ms** threshold from the roadmap, so a tap is not an utterance and a hold is. Total over its
  inputs, host-tested with a fake clock the way `pure/chrome.h`'s fade rules are.
- A release before the threshold is a **tap**, not a short utterance: a listening window that opened
  and closed in 80 ms would send `listen_start` and `listen_stop` with nothing between them, and
  v1.3 would have to special-case an empty utterance.
- **Glue:** `M5.Touch` press and release, fed to the machine every loop.
- The machine reports transitions rather than levels, so the caller sends `listen_start` exactly
  once per hold — an edge, not a poll.
- A touch that begins while the device is not idle (speaking, thinking, or in the console) is
  refused, and the refusal is visible on serial rather than silent.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
Press and hold on the face opens a listening window; release closes it; a tap does neither.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] A hold past the threshold produces exactly one start transition, and release exactly one stop
- [ ] A release before the threshold produces neither
- [ ] The machine is total over its inputs, including a release with no press
- [ ] **Unit test:** the transitions against a fake clock, under `pio test -e native`
- [ ] A touch outside `idle` is refused and says so

### RF-038 — The input-level meter

**Description:**
The only signal that the device is listening. Touches `firmware/src/pure/chrome.h` (the band rules),
`firmware/src/app/chrome_view.*` and `firmware/test/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- An envelope from the captured PCM — cheap, in the pure layer, since it is arithmetic over samples.
- The meter draws in the **bottom band**, per DEVICE_UI §Indicators, and the band is
  **single-tenant**: the carousel, a toast and the meter never share it, and the documented priority
  is **carousel > toast > level meter**. The meter yields; it does not overlap.
- **The word "listening" is never printed.** DEVICE_UI and MISSION both say a device state is a face
  or an indicator, never a label, and this is the indicator.
- It appears while listening and goes when listening ends — it is not subject to the ~3 s settle
  fade, because it is live rather than settled.

**Dependencies:** RF-036, RF-037

**Expected result:**
While you hold the face, the bottom band shows the level of what the microphone hears.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] The meter is visible only while listening
- [ ] It never draws while the carousel or a toast owns the band
- [ ] No text is drawn for it
- [ ] **Unit test:** the envelope maths and the band-priority rule, under `pio test -e native`

### RF-039 — `/loopback` — the capture smoke check

**Description:**
A serial command that captures a few seconds and plays it straight back, so the microphone can be
proved on its own before any of it depends on the network. Touches `firmware/src/main.cpp`.

**What needs to be done:**
- `/loopback [seconds]` — capture, then play back through the existing playback path.
- **A diagnostic, not a feature.** It is listed in `/help` as such and sends nothing to the server;
  MISSION's non-goals do not gain a voice recorder.
- It exercises the bus switch in both directions in one command, which is the part most likely to be
  wrong and the slowest to notice through the network path.

**Dependencies:** RF-036

**Expected result:**
`/loopback` records and replays, proving capture and the bus switching without a server.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `/loopback` captures and plays back audibly
- [ ] It is listed in `/help` and marked as a diagnostic
- [ ] Nothing is sent to the server by it
- [ ] The device returns to `idle` with the bus released afterwards

## v1.2 scope notes

**Total effort:** roughly 16–22 days across six issues; two S, three M, one L.
**Critical path:** RF-035 → RF-036 → RF-038 → RF-040.
**Phase DoD (roadmap §v1.2):** holding the face streams speech to the server **while you are still
speaking** (the server logs frames arriving before `listen_stop`, not after); releasing ends the
utterance; oversize utterances are cut with a clean error.
**Contracts pinned this phase:** the **listening window** — `listen_start` / binary `audio` /
`listen_stop`, the binary-phase rule that gives an inbound binary frame its meaning, and the
utterance cap. `ARCHITECTURE.md` and the contract test land with RF-035. The WS *vocabulary* is
unchanged: all three names were declared in v0.1.
**Model note:** chat runs on **Gemini 2.5 Flash only** (`thinkingBudget: 0`); **no paid APIs** — this
phase adds no provider call at all. The audio goes to the server and stops there until v1.3 adds
ASR; the integration test streams a canned PCM buffer from the fake device.
