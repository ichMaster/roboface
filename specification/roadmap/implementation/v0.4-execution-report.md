## What the version delivered

**A device state is now a face.** The screen shows an expression, never a word -- the only text this
firmware puts on a panel is an enumerated error code, which is an identifier rather than a state.

**`IFaceRenderer` is fixed while the renderer behind it is a stub**, which is the point of building a
stub at all: v2's animated renderer implements this interface rather than inventing one, and v2.6's
skins swap behind it. `setAudioLevel` exists and does nothing until v1 -- declaring it now is what
stops v1 widening the interface.

**Chrome's rules are pure and its pixels are glue.** The rules are almost entirely about time, so
they live where a fake clock proves the three-second settle in microseconds; on a board every one of
those assertions would mean watching a screen for four seconds and trusting what you saw.

## Three bugs, and where each was caught

1. **The chrome view drew invented facts** (RF-021). `draw()` passed `kConnected` and `0%` because
   `Chrome` exposed no way to read what it held -- it would have rendered "connected" arcs and an
   empty battery whatever was true, and looked entirely plausible doing it. Caught in review before
   the commit; `Chrome` now exposes its facts, with tests.
2. **The self-test lied about what it had done** (RF-022). The cycle printed *"back to the real
   state"* and left the device in `error`, so the status line disagreed with the screen
   indefinitely. Caught by **reading the serial output of the run**, not by reasoning about the code.
3. **`main.cpp` drew directly with `M5.Display`** -- carried in from v0.3, and the reason the DoD's
   "the renderer interface is the only way the app draws a face" was false until RF-022. Caught by
   the reconciliation step, before any code was written.

## The DoD -- met

Roadmap §v0.4: *"the board shows a distinct face for `idle`, `listening`, `thinking`, `replying`,
`offline` and `error`; link and battery appear when they matter and fade when they settle; a fault
shows the error face plus its code; the renderer interface is the only way the app draws a face;
v0 is a complete, self-contained skeleton."*

The `/faces` self-test is what made the first clause checkable at all: without a reachable server,
`idle`, `thinking` and `replying` have no natural route, and DEVICE_UI's own rule means the screen
will never tell you which one you are looking at.

## Architecture impact -- for the next reconcile

1. **`IFaceRenderer` is pinned**: `begin`/`show`/`setAudioLevel`/`tick`. v2.2 adds an `EmotionFrame`
   overload of `show`; the `DeviceState` form **stays**, because boot, offline and error are device
   facts and never emotions the server sends.
2. **`pure/face.h` is the recipe table's ancestor.** v2.2 changes the *source* of a recipe without
   changing the idea of one.
3. **`pure/layout.h` owns the screen geometry** and `clearOfFace` is asserted at compile time in the
   drawing code. Any later chrome must pass the same check.
4. **`pure/chrome.h` already arbitrates the bottom band** for tenants v1 and v2.6 will add.
5. **No `EmotionFrame` seam was touched.** v0.4's face is driven by `DeviceState` alone.
