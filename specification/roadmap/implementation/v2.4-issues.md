# v2.4 — GitHub Issues

Issues for phase **v2.4 — Touch, motion and proximity: the two-level model** (version **v2 — Living
face**), derived from the per-phase Tasks in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) (§v2.4) and the contracts
in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) (§Contracts, §event{}, §The face, §Interaction), with the
gesture map from [features/DEVICE_UI.md](../../features/DEVICE_UI.md) §Input. IDs continue from the
previous phase (RF-066 → **RF-067…RF-072**).

The phase's whole idea is **two levels with different deadlines**. A reflex answers the body in under
100 ms because a person who touches something expects it to notice *now*; the `event{}` that follows
lets the character answer in its own time, and may produce nothing at all. Neither substitutes for
the other, and the reflex must work with the server gone — which is the DoD's last clause and the
reason the two are separate mechanisms rather than one.

**Reconciled against the real implementation.** Four facts change what this phase has to build:

| | State | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `pure/ptt.h` | press-and-hold with a tap already classified (v1.2), `kTapped` in use | The gesture layer **extends** this rather than replacing it |
| The single tap | Toggles the microphone since v2.1 — a stop-gap | DEVICE_UI §Input records it as temporary and says mute moves to a two-finger tap "when the reflexes land". They land here. **RF-071** |
| `event{}` | Declared in `DeviceMessage`, no typed frame, no decoder | A seam to implement, with its contract test — **RF-068** |
| IMU / proximity | **No code at all.** `M5Unified` exposes both; nothing reads them | Genuinely new, and the pure/glue split has to be got right from the first line — **RF-069**, **RF-070** |

**What the roadmap asks for that this phase deliberately does not do.** §v2.4's Tasks mention the
hold converting to the v2.6 carousel past 1.2 s. The carousel is v2.6 and there is nothing to
convert *to* — so this phase implements the **detection** (a hold that passes 1.2 s without speech is
a distinct outcome) and leaves the destination to v2.6. Building half a carousel here would be a
later phase's scope arriving early, and building none would leave v2.6 to discover the hold cannot
tell the two apart.

## v2.4 — Touch, motion and proximity

### RF-067 — Touch zones and the gesture vocabulary

**Description:**
Where on the face a touch landed, and what the finger did. Pure logic over the panel's coordinates
and a clock; `pure/ptt.h` already classifies press/hold/tap and is extended rather than replaced.
Touches `firmware/src/pure/` and `firmware/test/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **Zones** over the face rectangle — cheek, forehead, eyes — as a pure function from a coordinate.
  Derived from `pure/layout.h`'s geometry rather than from fresh numbers, or a change to where the
  eyes are drawn would silently move where a poke is detected.
- **The gestures DEVICE_UI §Input names**: `tap`, `multi_tap`, `stroke`, `poke_eye`, `long_press`.
  A stroke is movement across zones while held; a poke is a press that lands on an eye; a multi-tap
  is taps within a window, and the **window is the design decision** — too short and a deliberate
  double tap reads as two singles, too long and two unrelated taps merge.
- **The hold's two outcomes**, layered by duration and intent as DEVICE_UI insists: PTT from ~120 ms,
  and a distinct "held past 1.2 s with no speech" outcome. v2.6 turns the second into the carousel;
  this phase only has to be able to *tell them apart*, and to keep speaking at any point making it a
  PTT hold.
- Host-tested from **recorded fixtures** — a sequence of (x, y, down, t) samples, generated from
  parameters the way v2.3's audio fixtures are, with the lesson from that phase applied: a fixture
  that does not resemble a finger proves nothing about fingers.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
A stream of panel coordinates becomes a stream of named gestures, on a laptop, with no `M5` include
anywhere near it.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** every gesture in DEVICE_UI §Input is produced by a fixture that represents it
- [ ] **Unit test:** a stroke and a multi-tap are told apart, and the boundary case (a slow drag with
      a pause) resolves to one of them deterministically
- [ ] **Unit test:** zones follow `layout.h` — a change to the eye geometry moves the poke zone
- [ ] **Unit test:** a hold is PTT before 1.2 s and a distinct outcome after, and speech keeps it PTT
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles; no `M5` include in `src/pure/`

### RF-068 — `event{}` end to end, and the seam it pins

**Description:**
The wire half of the second level: a typed `event{type, kind, meta}` frame, its decoder, its place in
the router, and the contract test that pins the enums. Touches
`server/roboface_server/{protocol,router}.py`, `firmware/src/pure/ws_protocol.h`, `tests/contract/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- The typed frame matching ARCHITECTURE §event{} exactly: `type` (`touch` · `motion` · `proximity`),
  `kind` (the per-type enum documented there), and a free-form `meta` object.
- **`meta` is free-form and that is deliberate** — but it is *bounded*: a device may not send an
  arbitrarily large object, and the cap belongs with the frame-size cap rather than being discovered
  by a memory error. Say so where the cap is.
- **An unknown `kind` is refused, not coerced.** This is the opposite decision from `emotion{}`, and
  the contrast is the point: a face is not worth dropping a connection over, but an *event* the
  server does not understand is a device claiming something the contract does not define, and
  silently mapping it to something else would let a firmware bug reach the character as a wrong
  reaction. `bad_frame`.
- The firmware's mirror: build the frame in `ws_protocol.h`, and the cross-tier check in
  `tests/contract/test_firmware_mirror.py` that the two `kind` vocabularies agree — the same drift
  guard v2.2 added for the emotion enum.
- The router accepts `event` while a turn is running: **a touch during a reply is the common case**,
  not an edge one, and rejecting it as out-of-state would make the phase's whole premise unreachable.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
A device can report what happened to it, and a malformed report is refused rather than misread.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Contract test:** the `event{}` shape and every `kind` enum, spelled literally
- [ ] **Contract test:** the two tiers' `kind` vocabularies agree, checked across C++ and Python
- [ ] **Unit test:** an unknown `type` or `kind` is `bad_frame`; an oversize `meta` is refused
- [ ] **Unit test:** `event` is accepted mid-turn and does not disturb the turn
- [ ] `ARCHITECTURE.md` §event{} matches the implementation, corrected in this commit if it does not

### RF-069 — The IMU, as pure logic over a raw stream

**Description:**
`tilt · shake · picked_up · upside_down · free_fall` from the BMI270's accelerometer, with every
decision in pure logic and only the sampling behind an `M5` include. Touches `firmware/src/pure/`,
a new glue module, and `firmware/test/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **A pure classifier fed (ax, ay, az) and a timestamp.** Gravity is the reference: at rest the
  vector's magnitude is ~1 g and its direction says which way is up. Tilt is that direction moving;
  free fall is the magnitude collapsing toward zero; upside down is it inverting; a shake is
  alternating sign at a rate no hand movement sustains by accident.
- **Thresholds derived and stated, not chosen.** Each one gets the number it came from — this project
  has spent three phases learning what a threshold picked by feel costs, and an IMU has five of them.
- **Hysteresis on every state**, for the reason `lipsync.h` documents: a value sitting on a threshold
  flips on adjacent samples, and a face that reacted to that would twitch.
- **Fixtures generated from parameters**: a still device, a slow tilt, a sharp shake, a drop, an
  inversion. The v2.3 lesson applies literally — a fixture that does not resemble the motion proves
  nothing about the motion, so each is built from the physics rather than from convenient numbers.
- Glue reads `M5.Imu` at a stated rate and feeds the classifier; nothing decides anything there.

**Dependencies:** RF-067

**Expected result:**
Picking the device up, tilting it, shaking it or turning it over each produce one named motion event,
and a device sitting on a desk produces none.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** each of the five motions is classified from a fixture built from its physics
- [ ] **Unit test:** a still device produces nothing over a long run — the false-positive case, which
      matters more than the others because it fires when nobody is there
- [ ] **Unit test:** every threshold has hysteresis, asserted the way `lipsync.h`'s is
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles; no `M5` include in `src/pure/`
- [ ] Manual DoD check deferred to the end-of-generation document

### RF-070 — Proximity: an approach wakes the face

**Description:**
The LTR-553 says how close something is. An approach wakes the face and biases its gaze; leaving
relaxes it. Touches a glue module, `firmware/src/pure/` for the decision, and `firmware/test/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **Pure:** approach and leave as a debounced two-state decision over a distance reading, with
  hysteresis — a hand hovering at the boundary must not produce a stream of events.
- **The gaze bias**, which is the first real use of `gaze` on the device: `EmotionFrame` has carried
  it since v2.2 and the renderer has ignored it. A local reflex overriding the server's gaze is
  exactly what ARCHITECTURE §Gaze describes, so this issue makes the field mean something and must
  say which wins.
- **Waking the face** is a reflex, not a state change: no `emotion{}` is involved and the server is
  not consulted. It works with the server gone, which is the DoD's last clause.
- Glue reads the sensor at a stated rate; the rate is a decision and carries its reason.

**Dependencies:** RF-067

**Expected result:**
A hand approaching the device is noticed within a reflex's deadline, and the face looks toward it.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** approach and leave, with hysteresis, from a distance-reading fixture
- [ ] **Unit test:** a hand held at the boundary produces one event, not a stream
- [ ] **Unit test:** a local gaze bias overrides the server's, and is released when the hand leaves
- [ ] `ARCHITECTURE.md` §Gaze records which source wins and when
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles; no `M5` include in `src/pure/`

### RF-071 — The reflex layer, and the tap goes back to affection

**Description:**
The first level: a short animation composited over whatever the face is currently doing, bounded and
non-blocking. And the correction DEVICE_UI has been waiting for since v1.4. Touches
`firmware/src/pure/`, `firmware/src/app/procedural_renderer.*`, `firmware/src/main.cpp`,
`specification/features/DEVICE_UI.md`.

**What needs to be done:**
- **A reflex is an overlay with a deadline.** It modulates the current recipe for a bounded time and
  then is gone; it does not target the crossfade, because a reflex that changed the expression would
  outlive itself and the face would keep whatever a poke did to it.
- **It never interrupts speech or listening** — roadmap §v2.4 says so twice, and it is the whole
  reason the two levels exist. The mouth keeps its viseme, the level meter keeps its band. Given what
  the last three phases cost, this wants a test rather than care.
- **Under 100 ms**, which is a claim about the *loop*, not about an animation's length: the reflex has
  to be visible on the next frame the renderer draws, and at 20 FPS that is 50 ms. Anything that
  waits for a network round trip has already failed the deadline by an order of magnitude.
- **The tap returns to affection, and mute moves to a two-finger tap.** DEVICE_UI §Input records the
  single-tap mute as temporary — *"the single tap belongs to the affection reflex; mute moves to two
  fingers when the reflexes land"*. They land here. `/mic on|off` stays: it is what makes the device
  scriptable, and the manual tests now depend on it.
- Repeated taps build joy, per DEVICE_UI. That is state with a decay, and it is pure.

**Dependencies:** RF-067, RF-068

**Expected result:**
Touching the face produces a visible reaction on the next drawn frame, and a reply in progress does
not notice.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** a reflex expires on its own and leaves the underlying expression untouched
- [ ] **Unit test:** a reflex during `replying` changes nothing about the mouth or the turn
- [ ] **Unit test:** repeated taps accumulate and decay
- [ ] The single tap is the affection reflex; mute is the two-finger tap; `/mic on|off` still works
- [ ] `DEVICE_UI.md` §Input loses the "v1.4, temporarily" note, because it stopped being true
- [ ] `pio run -e cores3` compiles

### RF-072 — The server answers a touch

**Description:**
The second level's far end: the server receives `event{}` and may answer with an `emotion` frame, a
spoken line, or nothing. Touches `server/roboface_server/{router,orchestrator,emotion}.py` and
`tests/integration/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- A handler that maps an event to a reaction. **"Or nothing" is a first-class outcome**: a device that
  is picked up and put down again does not need a line, and a character that comments on every touch
  is a character nobody wants on a desk.
- A spoken reaction goes through the **same turn machinery** as any other reply — the same
  orchestrator, the same emotion frame, the same TTS. A second path to speech would be a second place
  for every latency and cancellation rule to be got wrong.
- **An event during a turn does not start a second turn.** The device can be stroked mid-reply, and
  half-duplex means there is nowhere to put a second answer. It may still change the *face*.
- The reaction is a **server** decision, per MISSION: the device reports what happened and renders
  what it is told.

**Dependencies:** RF-068, RF-071

**Expected result:**
Stroking the face while the device is idle can produce a spoken reaction; doing it mid-reply does
not derail the reply.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Integration test:** an `event{}` from the fake device can produce an `emotion` frame and a
      spoken line, through the ordinary turn path
- [ ] **Integration test:** an event mid-turn does not start a second turn and does not disturb the
      first
- [ ] **Unit test:** an event the server chooses not to answer produces no frames at all
- [ ] **LLM mocked by default**; no paid call in the suite
- [ ] Ties to the phase DoD: the same inputs reach the server and can produce a spoken reaction

## v2.4 scope notes

**Total effort:** roughly 24 points (M+M+L+M+L+M) — the largest phase since v1, because it adds two
sensors that have no code at all and a second reaction level with its own deadline.

**Critical path:** RF-067 → RF-071 → RF-072.

**Phase DoD (roadmap §v2.4):** a tap, a stroke, a tilt, a shake and an approaching hand each produce
a visible local reaction within ~100 ms without disturbing an ongoing turn; the same inputs reach the
server and can produce a spoken reaction; **with the server offline, every reflex still works**.

**Contracts pinned this phase:** the **WS protocol** gains `event{}` with its `kind` enums (RF-068),
pinned from both sides and checked across the two languages. `EmotionFrame` does not change, but
`gaze` acquires its first consumer (RF-070) and ARCHITECTURE §Gaze must say which source wins.

**What this phase must not undo.** Three rules earned in v2.1–v2.3, each of which a reflex layer is
positioned to break: the face may drop frames but the microphone may not; a reflex must not touch the
mouth while the device is speaking; and anything that feeds an animation must answer *what happens
when it stops being updated*. The last one applies directly — a reflex is by definition a thing that
stops.

**Model note:** RF-072 is the only server work and it uses the existing `LLMProvider` seam
unchanged. **No paid APIs** — the mock answers, and a fake device sends the events.
