## The design, in one line each

**The seam is a session, not a call.** `ASRProvider.open() -> ASRSession`, with `push` and
`results` running at once. A `transcribe(audio) -> str` shape could only begin once the audio was
complete, which costs ~1.4 s at the worst possible moment -- after the person stops and starts
waiting. Encoding that in the seam means no implementation can accidentally undo it.

**The phrase-hold decides when a thought ended.** Deepgram's `speech_final` means "this span is
settled", not "the speaker stopped"; a breath mid-sentence produces one, usually without terminal
punctuation. Held for the continuation or the `UtteranceEnd` backstop. Answering it is the character
talking over someone, which reads as rudeness rather than as a bug.

**The ASR budget is on resolution, not recognition.** Everything before `listen_stop` happened while
the person was talking and cost them nothing. That is why the leg should be smallest, and why the
DoD is stated as a comparison between three numbers rather than a single total.

## Architecture impact

Two contracts pinned, each with its `ARCHITECTURE.md` update and contract test in the same commit:
the **`ASRProvider` seam** and the **`asr_partial`/`asr` frames**. All three vendor seams now exist
and none is shaped like another -- chat is a stream, TTS is a stream, ASR is a session -- because
each has a different relationship with time.

Three notes for v1.4:

1. **`endListening` no longer ends the turn.** v1.2 added that line with a comment saying v1.3 would
   remove it; it is removed. A reply ends the turn now.
2. **`open_listening` is optional on the `Responder` seam.** A responder without it is a text-only
   server, which is what a deployment with no Deepgram key still is.
3. **An empty utterance resolves to nothing and produces no reply.** v1.4's VAD will open windows
   without a finger involved and will produce far more of them.

## Not yet verified on hardware

Everything above is proven against mocks and an injected fake socket -- **no paid call in the
suite**. The end-to-end check with a real voice, a real Deepgram key and the three latency numbers
is RF-047's manual DoD, run after the pipeline completes.
