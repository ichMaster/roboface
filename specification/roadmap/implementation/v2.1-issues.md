# v2.1 — GitHub Issues

Issues for phase **v2.1 — Procedural face and the idle loop** (version **v2 — The living face**),
derived from the per-phase Tasks in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) (§v2.1) and the contracts in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) (§The face → Renderer ladder, §Firmware architecture).
This file is scoped to a single phase; IDs continue from the previous phase
(RF-053 → **RF-054…RF-058**).

v2.1 is where the product starts. Until now the screen has shown a stub; from here the face is
alive in silence — blinking, breathing, its gaze drifting — and a change of state crossfades rather
than snapping. Everything is driven by device state for now; the server's `EmotionFrame` channel
arrives in v2.2.

## Reconciliation notes (real code, not just the design)

Three things found by reading the current firmware, each of which changes what these issues contain:

- **The `IFaceRenderer` seam already exists and is not changed by this phase.** `face_renderer.h`
  pins exactly the four methods v2.1 needs — `begin`, `show(DeviceState)`, `setAudioLevel`, `tick` —
  and its own comment says why: *"an interface agreed against a stub is one later versions extend;
  an interface discovered while writing the real thing is one everything else has to be rewritten
  around."* v2.1 therefore **implements** the seam. No `ARCHITECTURE.md` seam update rides along,
  which is unusual for a phase this size and worth stating so nobody goes looking for one.
- **The recipe table is already written.** `pure/face.h` has `FaceRecipe` (eye openness, mouth
  curve, brow angle, tilt, dim), `recipeFor(DeviceState)` total over the enum, plus
  `recipeDistance` and `areDistinct`. v0.4 built it against the stub. The Tasks list says "the
  layer bank and the emotion recipe table as pure logic" — **half of that exists**, so RF-054 adds
  the layer bank over the recipe rather than starting from nothing.
- **`begin()` is specified to load the skin into PSRAM, and PSRAM now works.** It reported
  `size=0` from v1.1 until `v1.4.2` fixed the memory mode, leaving ~35 KB of internal RAM. There
  are now 8 MB of PSRAM and 263 KB internal. This phase is the first that can honour what
  ARCHITECTURE §Renderer ladder always said, and RF-057 depends on it outright: two 320×240
  sprites for a crossfade are 300 KB and could not have existed a day ago.

One more thing carried from v1, and it is why RF-058 exists as its own issue rather than as a note
at the end of RF-057: **on this board the display and the microphone compete for the same loop.**
Three separate times in v1 a repaint or a diagnostic starved capture — once costing 53% of every
window. v2.1 adds a continuous animation to a device that must keep hearing, which is the same
collision by design rather than by accident.

## v2.1 — Procedural face and the idle loop

### RF-054 — The layer bank: a recipe becomes geometry

**Description:**
A `FaceRecipe` says *what the face is doing* — eyes 85% open, mouth curved +0.2. It says nothing
about where anything is on a 320×240 screen. This issue is the layer between them: pure, in
`firmware/src/pure/`, and the reason the renderer that follows can be judged by looking at it rather
than by reading it.

**What needs to be done:**
- Add `firmware/src/pure/layers.h` — a small bank in the order ARCHITECTURE §The face names:
  background/glow, base, eyes, brows, mouth, overlay.
- A pure `layout(recipe, geometry) -> LayerBank` turning the five recipe floats into positions,
  sizes and colours. No drawing, no `M5` include, no floats left to the renderer to interpret.
- Eyes carry openness as a *height*, brows an angle as two endpoints, the mouth a curve as three
  control points — shapes the renderer draws without deciding anything.
- `dim` applies as a brightness scale over the whole bank rather than per layer, so a dimmed face
  cannot come apart into differently-lit pieces.
- Reuse `pure/layout.h`'s existing screen geometry rather than restating any coordinate.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
A recipe deterministically becomes a bank of drawable shapes, testable on a laptop.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `layout()` is total over every `FaceRecipe` the recipe table can produce.
- [ ] **Unit test:** the six layers are present and ordered; eye height tracks openness monotonically;
      a smile and a frown produce mirrored mouth control points; `dim` scales every layer equally.
- [ ] No `M5`/`Arduino` include reaches it; compiles under `pio test -e native`.
- [ ] Ties to §v2.1 Tasks: "the layer bank … as pure logic, separate from drawing".

### RF-055 — The idle loop: blink, breathe, drift

**Description:**
The phase's goal in one sentence — *the face is alive even in silence*. Three motions over the
layer bank: a blink every few seconds, a slow breathing bob, and a micro drift of the gaze. Pure,
driven by an injected clock, in `firmware/src/pure/`.

**What needs to be done:**
- `firmware/src/pure/idle.h` — an `IdleLoop` advanced by elapsed milliseconds, producing offsets the
  renderer applies over the bank: eye-openness multiplier, vertical bob, gaze offset.
- Blink: randomised interval within a band, ~120 ms closed. **Randomised but deterministic** —
  seeded, so a test can assert the whole sequence and a person still does not see a metronome.
- Breathe: a slow sine, amplitude of a few pixels. Gaze: a slower, smaller drift on two axes.
- All three scaled by `intensity`, so a later `EmotionFrame` can damp or exaggerate the idle
  without the loop knowing what an emotion is.
- No `millis()`: the caller passes time, exactly as `pure/vad.h` and `pure/ptt.h` do.

**Dependencies:** RF-054

**Expected result:**
An idle face that moves believably, and a loop a host test can run for an hour in a millisecond.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** deterministic under an injected clock — the same seed and the same timeline
      produce the same blinks, and blink intervals stay inside the configured band.
- [ ] A blink completes in the configured duration; the bob and drift stay within their amplitudes.
- [ ] `intensity` of 0 stills the face without stopping the loop; 1 gives full motion.
- [ ] Ties to §v2.1 Tests: "the idle scheduler is deterministic under an injected clock".

### RF-056 — Crossfade, and the relaxation back to neutral

**Description:**
A state change must not snap. ARCHITECTURE §Renderer ladder gives 150–250 ms of crossfade; the
roadmap adds `ttl_ms` relaxation toward `neutral`. Both are interpolation over `FaceRecipe`, which
makes them pure — and `recipeDistance` already exists to say how far apart two faces are.

**What needs to be done:**
- `firmware/src/pure/crossfade.h` — a `Crossfade` holding *from*, *to* and progress, advanced by
  elapsed time, yielding an interpolated `FaceRecipe`.
- Eased, not linear: a linear blend reads as a slide rather than a change of expression.
- A crossfade interrupted mid-flight starts from **where it currently is**, not from its original
  source — otherwise a quick sequence of states visibly jumps backwards before moving on.
- `ttl_ms` relaxation: after a target's time is up, drift back to the `kIdle` recipe over a longer,
  gentler curve than a state change uses.
- Use `areDistinct` to skip crossfades between faces too similar to be worth animating.

**Dependencies:** RF-054

**Expected result:**
Expression changes read as movement; a face left alone returns to rest on its own.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] **Unit test:** interpolation is continuous and monotonic in progress; the eased curve starts
      and ends at exactly the endpoint recipes.
- [ ] Interrupting a crossfade never produces a discontinuity in the output recipe.
- [ ] `ttl_ms` expiry relaxes to the idle recipe and stays there.
- [ ] Ties to §v2.1 Tasks: "crossfade (~150–250 ms) between recipes; `ttl_ms` relaxation".

### RF-057 — `ProceduralRenderer`: sprites, composited, on the panel

**Description:**
The glue: an `IFaceRenderer` that draws the layer bank with M5GFX sprites and replaces
`StubRenderer`. Touches `firmware/src/app/` only — every decision it needs was made by the three
pure issues above, and this one owns buffers, the panel and nothing else.

**What needs to be done:**
- `firmware/src/app/procedural_renderer.{h,cpp}` implementing the existing `IFaceRenderer`. **The
  seam does not change** — v0.4 fixed it against the stub precisely so this issue implements it.
- Sprites per layer where it earns the memory, composited and pushed. **PSRAM, not internal RAM**:
  it works as of v1.4.2, and internal RAM is where the I2S DMA buffers come from — v1 spent an
  evening on exactly that collision.
- `tick()` drives the idle loop and the crossfade; `show()` sets a target rather than drawing.
- Retire `StubRenderer` in the same commit; leaving two renderers behind one interface invites the
  question of which one is live.
- `begin()` returning false when it cannot allocate stays honest — a renderer that failed must say
  so rather than presenting a blank screen as a working one.

**Dependencies:** RF-054, RF-055, RF-056

**Expected result:**
The real face, on the panel, driven by device state.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] The face renders for every `DeviceState`, blinks, breathes and drifts when idle.
- [ ] `begin()` allocates from PSRAM and returns false rather than half-succeeding.
- [ ] Compiles for `cores3`; the pure layer keeps its host tests green with no `M5` include added
      to `src/pure/`.
- [ ] `StubRenderer` is gone, not merely unused.
- [ ] Ties to §v2.1 DoD: "on an idle device the face blinks, breathes and drifts its gaze;
      switching state crossfades rather than snapping".

### RF-058 — The frame budget: a face that does not cost the ears

**Description:**
Hold 15–20 FPS **with the audio pipeline running**, and prove it. Its own issue rather than a note
inside RF-057, because in v1 the display starved the microphone three separate times — once taking
53% of every capture window — and each time it was found late, by someone wondering why recognition
had stopped working.

**What needs to be done:**
- Measure and report frames per second and captured frames per second together, in the existing
  status line. Two numbers side by side, because either alone hides the trade.
- A repaint cadence that yields to capture: the face may drop frames, the microphone may not.
- Verify on the board with active listening on — a full hands-free turn while the face animates,
  with capture staying at ~50 frames/second.
- Record the measured numbers in the execution report, not just "it felt smooth".

**Dependencies:** RF-057

**Expected result:**
A face that animates continuously without costing a single captured audio frame.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] 15–20 FPS sustained with audio running; capture stays ≥ 95% of the window.
- [ ] Both numbers visible in the status line, so a regression is noticed rather than discovered.
- [ ] No tearing at the target rate.
- [ ] Ties to §v2.1 DoD: "no tearing and no audio glitches at target FPS".

## v2.1 scope notes

**Total effort:** ~17 points (1 L, 4 M) — roughly three weeks at the sizes above.
**Critical path:** RF-054 → RF-057 → RF-058.
**Phase DoD (roadmap §v2.1):** on an idle device the face blinks, breathes and drifts its gaze;
switching state crossfades rather than snapping; the recipe table covers the full emotion enum; no
tearing and no audio glitches at target FPS.
**Contracts pinned this phase:** none newly — `IFaceRenderer` already exists and v2.1 implements it
rather than changing it. The recipe table's totality over the enum is already pinned by v0.4's
tests and stays pinned.
**Model note:** chat runs on **Gemini 2.5 Flash only** (`thinkingBudget: 0`) behind the
`LLMProvider` seam; **no paid APIs** — every provider seam is mocked and a fake device drives the
protocol for unit + integration tests. This phase touches none of them: it is firmware and a screen.
**What v2.1 deliberately does not do:** the server's `EmotionFrame` channel (v2.2), lip-sync from
the playback envelope (v2.3), and the skins (v2.6). The renderer is built so all three are
additions rather than rewrites — which is what the seam was fixed for in v0.4.
**Carried in from v1:** the display and the microphone share one loop and this board has no
headroom to spare. RF-058 exists because of it.
